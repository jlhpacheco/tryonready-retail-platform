# IP and License Boundary

## Ownership intent

TryOnReady is being created as an original, standalone project owned by the repository owner unless a later written agreement provides otherwise.

The repository must not contain materials owned by an employer, Duevara, another company, a customer, a teammate, or a contractor unless the owner has written authorization and the applicable license is documented.

## Sponsor relationship

Submitting TryOnReady to the hackathon does not transfer ownership of the project. The submission may be used for judging and may be promoted under the applicable hackathon and Devpost terms.

Treat all submitted code, screenshots, descriptions, and videos as non-confidential disclosure materials.

## Repository licensing

- No open-source license is granted by default.
- Absence of a license does not authorize public copying, modification, or redistribution.
- Do not add MIT, Apache-2.0, GPL, BSD, Creative Commons, or another license without explicit owner approval.
- Third-party packages remain governed by their own licenses.

## Third-party components

Before adding a package, asset, font, icon, image, video, or dataset:

1. Verify that its license permits the intended use.
2. Record it in `THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md`.
3. Preserve required notices or attribution.
4. Do not use content with unclear or incompatible rights.

## Images and media

Permitted demonstration assets are limited to:

- Synthetic assets
- Assets created and owned by the repository owner
- Assets with explicit written permission
- Assets licensed for the intended commercial and promotional use

Do not assume that an image found online is free to use.

## Contributors

Do not add a teammate or contractor until ownership is clear in writing. Contributions should be made under terms confirming that the contributor has the authority to provide the work and that the project owner may use, modify, commercialize, and submit it.

## Separation from Duevara

TryOnReady must not reuse or disclose Duevara Core, proprietary workflows, private algorithms, architecture, data models, credentials, security controls, customer information, or branding.
