## Continuous narration draft

### 0:00–0:22 — The resource gap

Independent boutiques compete for the same shoppers as national platforms, without separate engineering, security, and catalog teams. Census data show nearly thirty-two thousand U.S. clothing-store firms have fewer than twenty employees. In this synthetic scenario, Elena Rivera runs Luna & Thread with one family member and a close friend. TryOnReady gives this three-person boutique virtual try-on it can actually operate.

### 0:22–0:42 — Guided boutique application

Before any shopper uploads a photo, Elena completes a guided boutique application, confirms her rights to the garment imagery, and identifies who will manage the service. The workflow is designed for a busy shop floor; provider credentials remain inside the secure application.

### 0:42–1:02 — Garment upload and readiness

She uploads the Moonlight Blazer. TryOnReady checks its dimensions and readiness before publication, protecting the catalog from unsuitable inputs and reducing wasted provider work. The garment stays unavailable until those checks pass.

### 1:02–1:20 — Human approval

An administrator reviews the boutique and garment. Human approval is a deliberate checkpoint. Elena prepares the item once; only then does it enter the private guest journey.

### 1:20–1:43 — Private guest path

Marisol enters as a guest, with no account required. She selects the approved blazer, reads the privacy and visualization limits, gives explicit consent, and supplies an authorized synthetic photo. The provider key stays on the ASP.NET server, and Marisol's image never appears in this demonstrated retailer view.

### 1:43–2:00 — Full-body controlled result

Here is Marisol's previously completed YouCam AI Clothes v3 result, replayed without a new provider request. The reveal turns uncertainty into a clear visual choice. It visualizes appearance—not physical fit or sizing.

### 2:00–2:10 — Optional repeatability beat

The same approved workflow can support Danielle's teal dress without changing the readiness, approval, privacy, or usage controls. Marisol remains the primary story.

If Danielle is omitted, retain the narration and use a clean four-step workflow montage instead. Do not shorten or retime the narration merely to force in a second model.

### 2:10–2:23 — Duplicate cost control

If the same authorized request is repeated, TryOnReady recognizes it before creating a second provider task, preventing duplicate unit use.

### 2:23–2:42 — Retailer-safe proof

Back at Luna & Thread, Elena sees one previously completed demonstration, active usage controls, duplicates stopped, and zero shopper photos in this retailer view. YouCam provides the apparel visualization engine. TryOnReady supplies the readiness, approval, consent, duplicate control, unit accounting, and privacy workflow around it.

### 2:42–2:50 — Measured next step

Next comes a measured boutique pilot, testing reliability, staff effort, shopper completion, controlled cost, and privacy compliance—only after human approval.

## Studio-quality acceptance gate

- Sequence master: 1920×1080 progressive, one constant approved frame rate matching the primary screen capture; no mixed-rate cadence defects.
- Capture: fresh complete takes, crisp native-resolution UI, deliberate cursor motion, no notifications, personal desktop content, browser chrome clutter, loading mistakes, or visible credentials.
- Edit: motivated clean cuts; restrained push-ins only where they improve legibility; no gimmick transitions, artificial slow motion, stretched footage, jumpy scrolling, or cuts during clicks and confirmations.
- Narration: one continuous natural professional performance, recorded before picture lock; consistent tone, distance, cadence, and room sound; no stitched phrase endings or forced gaps.
- Audio preflight: clear speech throughout, no clipping, clicks, hum, abrupt edits, or masking; final loudness and true peak measured once the delivery platform and master format are locked.
- Captions: verbatim to the approved narration, human-reviewed, maximum two lines and 42 characters per line, at least 4.5:1 contrast, safe-area compliant, and never covering faces, garments, controls, or evidence labels.
- Picture QA: readable evidence at normal playback size; correct white balance and exposure for people/assets; full-body frames retain head, hands, hem, and feet; no offline media, accidental scaling, or low-resolution enlargement.
- Story QA: emotional warmth comes from the synthetic boutique story and full-body reveal, while every technical claim remains attached to visible evidence.
